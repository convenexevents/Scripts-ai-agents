## 2. New venue-specific copy block (226 words)

For a City audience, The Ned keeps its private rooms together on one restored floor, which keeps arrivals simple and the evening contained.

**The Rose Room** is the one for a small, senior table. At 21 m², it takes up to 14, and is named after the Rose Tavern, which stood on the site before the Great Fire of London. Original chandeliers, a marble fireplace and mahogany panelling make it feel like a private study.

**The RWB Room**, at 45 m² with light oak panelling, has an intimate, informal feel and takes up to 50, so it suits a dinner of 20 to 30 without crowding.

**The Drawing Room**, at 70 m², has a club-like feel, with restored panelled walls lined with period etchings, paintings and lithographs, and connects to **The Saloon**, known for an 18th-century chandelier from London's Devonshire House. The largest room, **the Tapestry Room**, has original walnut panelling and crystal chandeliers and holds up to 200. **The Princes Dome**, one of two converted domes on the roof, has its own outdoor terrace.

For finance, treasury and risk leaders who work in the City, the Rose Room is a natural home for a confidential dinner of 10 to 14. The RWB Room or Drawing Room suits a larger cross-sector evening. The Princes Dome adds a terrace for a summer drinks welcome.
