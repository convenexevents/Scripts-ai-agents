## 1. The idea

### Campaign idea: "The other side of the table"

Almost everything written about executive events is written for the organiser or the sponsor, and most of it is about headcount, badge scans and "pipeline". This week we write from the other side: the senior guest deciding whether to give up an evening, and the host who chooses to listen instead of pitch. Each day takes one gap nobody owns (Nadia, section 3) and makes one concrete point about it.

### The line that runs through the week

> **Who's in the room is the event.**

How it runs:
- It's the closing line on both carousels and the tagline strip on every single-image post (same position, same type).
- Theo works it, or a close echo of it, into the payoff of each text post. It doesn't need to appear word for word in every caption, but every post should prove it from a different angle.
- It's the bridge to the wedge in the brand brief (a vetted guest list the host signs off, plus a properly run conversation, beats a badge-scan) without saying "no slides, no pitch", which Nadia lists as overused.

### Why this, why now
- **Carousels are an open lane.** Theo's 7-day scan found 0 carousels in the on-topic top 20 and only 16 documents across 562 posts. We run 2.
- **Individuals outperform company pages** (median score 6 against 3 in Theo's pool). Nathan's first-person variants carry the reach; the company page is the archive and the place the site links live.
- **Planning season.** Hosts are setting 2027 budgets now (Theo's 30-day benchmark). The 12-week post lands on Friday with a concrete date.
- **Search demand backs the topics.** "executive dinner" is the site's biggest cluster, "b2b virtual roundtable" has 331 impressions at position 9, and event ROI has no page at all (Dev, section 2).

### Readers across the week
- Would-be hosts (marketing and sales leaders at B2B tech and finance software firms): Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri.
- Senior guests (Directors and C-level): Mon, Sat.
- Both: Sun, plus the comment questions every day.

This keeps the feed from reading as 7 days of promotion (Theo's advice: mix the reader).

## 3. Day-by-day plan

Rules for every day (Theo and Felix):
- UK English, contractions, digits, no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical-question openers, nothing from the brief's banned list.
- No personal names of anyone at Convene X, in the company posts or the personal variants. No event counts, capacity rates or "in the last X months". Proof only as "the team in previous roles" (company) or "in previous roles, I..." (personal), with no numbers attached.

- Hooks must fit before the "see more" cut (about 110 to 140 characters on mobile) and pass the first-3-words test.
- Site links go in the **first comment**, never the post body (brand brief). The post ends with "Link in the first comment."
- End on a real question to pull comments (every comment-heavy post in Theo's set did).
- 0 to 2 hashtags (Theo found more doesn't help).
- Stats only from Nadia's "safe to use" table, with the source named in the post or on the image. Vendor data is labelled as a vendor survey.
- Company and personal versions are separate drafts in separate voices. The personal variant isn't a reshare of the company post.

- **Key points:**
  1. Who else is coming, by role and seniority, not by name. A room of peers is the reason to say yes.

### Tuesday 17 Nov: The host who doesn't pitch

- **Theme:** The host's real job at the table is to stay quiet.
- **Goal:** Comments from would-be hosts (this is the argument post of the week). Secondary: dinners page visits.
- **Reader:** Would-be hosts.
- **Format:** Text only, about 180 to 230 words (Theo's top text posts sat around this length).
- **Hook (Theo's hook 1):** "The best sponsored dinner is the one where the sponsor doesn't pitch."
- **Key points:**
  1. Buyers don't dislike people; they dislike being sold to. Gartner found 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience (Gartner, 9 Mar 2026), and 69% turn to sales reps to validate AI-generated insights (Gartner, 20 May 2026). They want expertise without the pitch. A room of peers gives them that.
  2. The moment it goes wrong is usually mid-dinner, when the conversation turns to a problem the host's product solves. That's the moment the host keeps quiet.
  3. What the host does instead: opens with the table question, introduces people to each other, speaks last, and follows up afterwards with something useful, not a deck.
  4. The relationship is earned by the quality of the evening, not by airtime.
- **CTA (soft):** "If you've hosted one of these, what was the hardest part of staying quiet?"
- **Link (first comment):** `https://convenexevents.com/private-executive-dinners/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=cx-week-2026-11-16&utm_content=tue-company`
- **Personal variant:** "I've watched a host lose a room with one slide." Then what that looked like from the guest's chair, and what the hosts who kept the room did differently. "In previous roles" framing, no counts, no company or person named. Same Gartner stat optional (one stat at most). Link `utm_content=tue-personal`.
- **Suggested time:** personal 15:00 (slot B); company page 11:00.
- **Visual:** none (text only). If Felix or Freya want an image, a plain brand-kit quote card of the hook, no stock photo.
- **Felix check:** the Gartner figures are 2 separate surveys; keep both dates and don't merge them into one claim.

- **Hook (Theo's hook 5):** "Judge an executive dinner 6 weeks after it ends, not on the night."
- **Key points:**
  1. The leadership gap is real: about half of organisations say their events perform well, but only 38% of CMOs agree (Forrester, The Next Era of B2B Events, 25 Jun 2026).
  2. Before the dinner: agree the 3 or 4 things worth looking at. Decide them up front, not after.
  3. On the night: did the right people come, measured against the signed-off list? Did the conversation go somewhere?
  4. Week 1: a follow-up that continues the conversation rather than selling.
  5. Weeks 2 to 4: who replied, who asked for a next conversation, who introduced a colleague.
  6. Week 6: where the relationships stand, and whether guests would come again.
  7. What we won't do: promise leads, meetings or ROI. These are things to look at, not outcomes anyone can guarantee.
