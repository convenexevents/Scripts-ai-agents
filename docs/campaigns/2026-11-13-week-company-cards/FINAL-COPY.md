A conference gives you reach. A small room gives you depth.

Both have a job. Conferences put you in front of a crowd. A small room puts you at a table with a handful of senior people, one question and no pitch.

## Post 6. Gartner: the host who doesn't pitch (from the signed-off 16 to 22 Nov week, reopened)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Date and time** | Tue 17 Nov, 11:00 |
| **Format** | Text only, company page |
| **First comment** | https://convenexevents.com/private-executive-dinners/ |
| **Card file** | None (text only) |
| **Nadia** | Both figures verified against Gartner's releases. Wording changed as below. |

What changed from the signed-off text: the 69% said "turn to sales reps to validate", and Gartner says "prefer to validate". The two figures were called "a separate survey", but both come from the same Gartner buyer research (fieldwork Aug to Sep 2025). "Read together" now labels our reading as ours.

```
The best sponsored dinner is the one where the sponsor doesn't pitch.

Buyers still want people in the conversation. What they avoid is being sold to.

Gartner found 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience (Gartner, 9 Mar 2026). In the same buyer research, 69% said they prefer to validate AI-generated insights with sales reps (Gartner, 20 May 2026). Fieldwork for both ran in Aug to Sep 2025.

Our reading: buyers still want expertise. They want it without the pitch. A table of peers gives them that, as long as the host lets it.

The moment it goes wrong is usually halfway through the main course. The conversation turns to a problem the host's product solves, and the host leans in.

That's the moment to stay quiet.

What a good host does instead:

1. Opens with the table question, then hands it over.
2. Introduces guests to each other, not to the product.
3. Speaks last.
4. Follows up afterwards with something useful, not a deck.

The relationship comes from the quality of the evening, not from airtime. Guests remember who gave them a good conversation. They also remember who turned it into a demo.

If you've hosted one of these, what was the hardest part of staying quiet?

Link in the first comment.
```
