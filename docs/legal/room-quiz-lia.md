# Legitimate Interests Assessment: following up room quiz leads (internal)

**Processing covered:** storing a quiz submitter's name, work email, job title, company and quiz result in the Convene X CRM sheet, sending the result email, one follow-up message about the result or a call, and preparing for a call they book. Upcoming-rooms emails are out of scope: they rely on consent.

**1. Purpose test.** Our interest is commercial: responding to business enquiries and finding hosts and guests for our rooms. The person started the contact, either by asking for their result or by booking a call. That interest is legitimate and clearly stated at the point of collection.

**2. Necessity test.** We need a name and work email to send the result and follow up. Job title and company are collected only for Book a call, where they're needed to prepare a useful conversation. Answers stay in the browser unless the person submits. Analytics don't capture identity, room or score. One follow-up is the least we can do to act on the enquiry.

**3. Balancing test.** The data is business contact data, not special category data. The people are senior professionals using work addresses, acting in a business capacity. They'd reasonably expect a result email and one follow-up. Impact is low: at worst, one unwanted email. Risk rises if follow-up is repeated, if data goes to sponsors, or if records are kept indefinitely.

**4. Safeguards.**
- Clear notice at the point of collection, with a link to the privacy policy
- Follow-up limited to once; further marketing only to opt-in Yes
- Unsubscribe in every email; objections added to the do-not-contact list straight away
- Deletion 24 months after last contact for non-clients
- No sharing of quiz data with sponsors or other third parties beyond the named processors
- Access to the sheet limited to people who need it

**5. Conclusion.** Legitimate interests is an appropriate basis for this processing, provided the safeguards above are in place. Review in 12 months, or sooner if the quiz, the follow-up pattern or the processors change.
