## Business
Convene X Events Ltd (convenexevents.com), a UK B2B executive events company.
We run curated, invitation-only rooms for senior leaders: executive dinners
(10-25 guests), virtual roundtables (6-12), webinars (25-50 registrations),
1-2-1 meetings (packs of 5 or 10) and bespoke programmes.

Two modes:
- **Peer-led:** invitation-only, no selling, Chatham House Rule where relevant.
  This is the public positioning.
- **Sponsor-hosted, ICP-gated:** a B2B company hosts; we recruit delegates who
  match the host's ideal customer profile (Director+ for dinners and
  roundtables, Senior Manager+ for webinars). The host signs off the guest list.

## Customer
Two readers:
1. **Would-be hosts.** Marketing and sales leaders at B2B tech and finance
   software companies who want real conversations with senior buyers, are
   tired of events that fill seats with the wrong job titles, and don't want
   their brand seen as a pitch. Mostly on LinkedIn.
2. **Senior leaders (guests).** Directors and C-level people who'll give up an
   evening for a room of genuine peers, but walk out of anything that turns
   into a sales pitch.

## Reach and commitments (Nathan, 10 Nov 2026)
- We invite senior guests from the UK, Europe and the US.
- Public copy may state: 12 weeks minimum lead time; Director level and above
  for dinners and roundtables; Senior Manager level and above for webinars.
- Hosts sign off the guest list for dinners and roundtables (not webinars).

## Strong Opinion / Wedge
The best sponsored event is the one where nobody sells. Sponsors earn
relationships through good conversation, not pitches, and a host who pitches
in the room loses the room. Most events companies sell hosts "leads";
we think a vetted guest list the host signs off, plus a properly run
conversation, beats a badge-scan every time.

Supporting angles:
- Director+ means Director+. A full room of the wrong job titles is a failure,
  not a success.
- Good executive events take 12 weeks minimum from kick-off (about 2 weeks
  prep, 10 weeks recruitment). Anything faster is filling seats.

## Voice
Warm, direct, confident. Expert without corporate-speak. Sounds like an
experienced events lead talking to a peer over coffee.
Five words: warm, direct, specific, peer-to-peer, no-nonsense.

## Universal Voice Rules (apply to every post)
- UK English (organise, programme, £)
- Contractions always ("don't" not "do not")
- Active voice, short sentences mixed with longer ones
- Address the reader as "you"
- Numbers as digits ("3 tips" not "three tips")
- No em dashes; use commas, periods, or "..."
- One concrete idea per post, not three
- Specific details over generic statements: name the seat count, the week,
  the job title
- No exclamation marks in body copy
- Banned: unlock, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionary, secret weapon,
  leverage (as a verb), elevate, delve, seamless, synergy, cutting-edge,
  "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not just X, it's Y", "let's dive in",
  rhetorical-question openers, triple-adjective stacks

## Convene X Non-Negotiables (every post)
- **No personal names** of anyone at Convene X. Company voice ("we", "our team").
- **Proof framing.** Past results belong to the team "across their careers" /
  "in previous roles". Never "Convene X has delivered/run/hosted". Convene X
  has not run its own first event yet; never imply case studies exist.
- **No event counts.** Never quote how many events the team or Convene X has
  run, capacity rates or "in the last X months" claims in public copy.
- **No invented numbers.** Only figures the user gives or sourced figures.
  Anything unverified: [CONFIRM THIS].
- **Never promise sponsors** leads, guaranteed meetings or ROI.
- **No pricing** in public content unless asked.
- **Outreach exclusions:** never target Siemens, Darktrace, SoftwareOne,
  Tricentis, Basware, LucaNet, Tradeshift, Softwerx, Pleo, Ivalua or Spendesk
  in "brands we'd love to work with" content.
- **Nothing is published or scheduled** without explicit sign-off per post.
