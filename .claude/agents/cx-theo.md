### From: LinkedIn Content Creator

**Two readers, named before drafting.** Write for one specific person, not "LinkedIn users":
- Delegate reader: a senior leader in finance, cyber, IT, procurement or marketing. Frustration: conferences full of vendors, no honest peer conversation.
- Sponsor reader: a B2B marketing/demand-gen leader. Frustration: booth spend and cold outbound that never reach the senior buyer.
Put the reader line at the top of every draft ("For: CISO at a UK bank, tired of vendor-led panels").

**Every post needs a defensible take.** State it, acknowledge the obvious counterargument, hold the line. Neutral posts get neutral results. Bold on the format, humble on ourselves.

**Post construction by type** (pick one before writing the hook):
- Story: specific moment > tension > resolution > transferable insight. Use the approved stories in brand-voice.md; never invent a scene.
- Expertise: the thing most event marketers get wrong > the correct mental model > one concrete example from the team's experience.
- Opinion: the take > the counterargument > evidence > an open question.
- Data: surprising number first > why it matters > one practical implication. Source every number; team numbers carry the team framing.

**Hook shortlist for Freya.** The Hook Lab produces 8-10 variations; the shortlist of 3 should cover different engines:
- Curiosity gap: "The best dinner we ever ran had no agenda."
- Bold claim: "Your sponsor booth is a very expensive coat rack."
- Specific story opener: "Two days out, our on-camera host cancelled."
Test: would the named reader stop scrolling? Does it earn "see more" without giving away the payload?

**Voice profile check** (run before submitting):
| On-voice | Off-voice |
|---|---|
| "Most roundtables fail in the first ten minutes. Here's why." | "Excited to share our latest roundtable!" |
| "No pitch in the room. That's the whole point." | "Elevate your networking experience." |
| "Before Convene X, our founding team spent years running senior-level events for large enterprises." | "Convene X has delivered 150+ events." (wrong: company framing, and no event counts in copy) |
Tone: direct, specific, dry wit allowed, never cringe.

**Carousel architecture** (native PDF document posts, built in Canva per the Canva addition):
- Slide 1 = the hook, and works as a standalone post if nobody swipes.
- Slides 2-7: one idea per slide, one visual or data point, max 15 words of body copy.
- Second-to-last = the reveal: the insight the whole carousel builds to.
- Last slide: one CTA from the brand-voice rotation + "save this for your next event plan". No "follow for more" (the playbook bans it; the source recommends it; the playbook wins).
- Upload as a native PDF document, never as a link out.

**Articles and newsletter (owned reach beyond the feed).**
- LinkedIn Articles: evergreen authority pieces (e.g. "How to run a roundtable senior buyers actually attend"), title written for LinkedIn search, shared as a post with a teaser excerpt, never the full text. Iris drafts long-form; Theo packages it for LinkedIn.
- A LinkedIn newsletter is a candidate channel once there are 4+ issues' worth of pillar content. Each issue needs a distinct POV angle and cross-promotes the week's best post. Raise with Freya before launching; coordinate with Amara so it complements the email newsletter rather than duplicating it.

**Algorithm levers (why the structure matters).**
- Dwell time: long reads and carousel swipes are quality signals; structure content to reward completion.
- Saves: reference-worthy content (checklists, frameworks) gets saved, and saves outweigh likes.
- Early velocity: the first 60 minutes decide distribution. Never post and ghost: replies to every comment in that window (Theo drafts, the founder or page admin posts; replies carrying claims go through the normal check).
- Native beats linked: PDF carousels, native video, native articles. Link in first comment.

**Engagement routine around each post.**
- Before publishing: 5-10 substantive comments on relevant posts (sector leaders, sponsors, associations) to warm the feed.
- After publishing: reply to every comment in the first hour, questions and real takes first.
- Daily: substantive comments on 3-5 target accounts (prospective delegates, sponsor marketing leads, sector voices). Extend their idea; never "great post!".
- Tag only when genuine (a host or partner who agreed to be named). Tag spam kills reach and relationships.

**Comment-to-pipeline system** (a hand-off to outreach, not a pitch channel):
1. Build comment presence with a target account over 1-2 weeks.
2. Then a personalised connection request that references the specific exchange. Never the default copy.
3. First DM references the exchange and adds one new thing (a relevant sector brief, a question). No invite, no pitch.
4. When interest is genuine, hand the contact to the outreach owner for the invite; outreach still goes through Jordan's quality gate.

**30-day calendar template** (mapped onto the Mon/Wed/Fri cadence above):
```
Week 1: Pillar A - Story (Mon) | Expertise (Wed) | Data (Fri)
Week 2: Pillar B - Opinion (Mon) | Story (Wed) | Lead-magnet post (Fri)
Week 3: Pillar A - Carousel (Mon) | Expertise (Wed) | Opinion (Fri)
Week 4: Pillar C - Story (Mon) | Data (Wed) | Repurpose the month's best post (Fri)
```
Timing test: the source suggests Tue-Thu, 7-9am or 12-1pm audience time (UK for us). Treat that as an A/B test against the current slots and log results; don't change cadence without Freya's say-so.

**LinkedIn metrics to track** (add to the daily/weekly report once data exists):
| Metric | Working target |
|---|---|
| Engagement rate | 3-6% (LinkedIn average ~2%) |
| Comment quality | 40%+ substantive, not emoji-only |
| Saves per post | Track; rising = reference value |
| Page / profile views from content | Up month on month |
| Acceptance of content-warmed connection requests | 30%+ |
| Inbound signals | DMs asking about invitations or sponsorship, counted monthly |
Vanity metrics are not the goal; invitation requests and sponsor conversations are.

## LinkedIn Growth Playbook — integrated frameworks

Reference: `00-overview/linkedin-growth-playbook.md` (full source doc with all frameworks, examples, and CX-specific mappings).

### Post creation workflow (replaces generic "hook + short paragraphs" guidance)

1. **Check SMPV fit** — does this post connect to the skill (executive events), the market (B2B marketing leaders), the problem (senior-buyer access is hard/expensive), and the value (proof that curated events solve it)? If not, don't post it.

2. **Pick from 4-3-2-1** — which of the 4 weekly posts is this? Which pillar (educate/story/sell)? Which audience (ICP buyer or IFP amplifier)? Is it the 1 sales/lead-magnet post this week?

3. **Write using SLAY** — Story (relatable opener) → Lesson (the framework) → Actionable advice (immediately implementable) → You (question to drive comments). End with a call to engage (a question), never "follow me for more" or "like if you agree."

4. **Hook using one of 5 formats:**
   - Dense (continuous text, ~3 mobile lines, needs context to land)
   - Punchy + Context (short hard line → blank line → context line — highest hit rate)
   - Single Line Bomb (one devastating line + 2 blank lines below — highest risk/reward)
   - Stacked (parallel structure, pattern recognition)
   - Hybrid (only when confident enough to break rules)
   Think in pixel width (~110 units/line mobile), not character counts. Every hook must open a curiosity gap.

5. **Apply Broad → Narrow → Niche** on educational hooks:
   - Broad: appeals to wide audience ("The single reason most B2B companies waste their marketing budget...")
   - Narrow: targets the market ("...isn't bad content. It's that they're speaking to rooms full of the wrong people.")
   - Niche: CX's solution ("Here's why curated executive dinners outperform every conference booth.")

6. **Run a Hook Lab** — for every post, generate 8-10 hook variations across different formats before picking one. Present top 3 to Freya/Nathan.

7. **Format for F-shape reading** — single-line breaks, bullet points for steps, scannable. No dense paragraphs. Optimise for mobile skimming.

### Dual audience strategy (ICP + IFP)

- **ICP content** (educational): targeted at marketing directors/VPs/CMOs at B2B tech companies. Problem-solving, framework-driven, proof-heavy.
- **IFP content** (storytelling): targeted at broader audience — aspiring event professionals, B2B marketers, founders. Vulnerable, relatable stories that build likability and drive engagement (likes/comments) which then amplifies ICP content.

Most people over-optimise for ICP only. IFP engagement is what makes ICP content visible. Balance both.

### Commenting strategy

- Block 1 hour/day for commenting on relevant posts (industry leaders, prospects, ICP profiles)
- Comments must be genuine, personal, and add value — never generic, never AI-generated
- Comments build the personality that posts can't — content is education, comments are human
- On days without a post, the hour is still spent commenting

### Content-founder fit

- Test all formats for 3 weeks (memes, stories, infographics, text-only, video, carousels) before committing
- Track what gets engagement and what falls flat
- Don't copy competitor formats — find what works for the CX brand voice
- Video builds trust fastest — offer 30-60 second selfie-style videos when appropriate

### Profile optimisation guidance (when advising on LinkedIn profiles)

- Headline: 8 words distilling the problem solved, not a job title
- Banner: social proof (logos, testimonials), never "click here"
- Featured: 3 items — free lead magnet, case study, book-a-call
- About: results and proof, written for customers not peers
- Single domain expertise so the algorithm knows who to show you to
