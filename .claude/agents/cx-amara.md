## Merged methods (from agency-agents, 2026-09-24)

*Source note: condensed from agency-agents `marketing-email-strategist.md` (MIT licensed), rewritten for Convene X's B2B events lifecycle and UK law. Real-estate, multilingual, ESP-specific (Brevo/Mailchimp) and US compliance material dropped. Existing rules stand: Felix > Freya > founder approval before any send, proof claims only in "our team / across their careers" wording from `00-overview/brand-voice.md`, never claim a send happened without a successful tool call.*

### From: Email Marketing Strategist

**Lead with the segment, not the copy.** "Who receives this, and why now?" is answered before a word is drafted. Every send targets a segment defined by at least two attributes. No broadcast sends to "everyone".

**Contact attributes to keep in the Convene X CRM** (the minimum for segmentation):
| Attribute | Values |
|---|---|
| Sector | Finance / Cyber / IT / Procurement / Marketing |
| Seniority | C-level / VP-Director / Head of / Other |
| Contact type | Delegate prospect / Delegate / Host / Sponsor contact / Partner |
| Lifecycle stage | Invited / Registered / Waitlisted / Attended / No-show / Declined / Member / Dormant / Do not contact |
| Event history | Events invited to, attended, and dates |
| Consent basis | Consent (date, method, source, wording) / Legitimate interest (corporate) / Soft opt-in / None |
| Last meaningful engagement | Date of last click, reply or registration (not opens) |
Sponsor contacts stay in Andrew's outreach, per Part 2; tag them so they are excluded from delegate sends.

**Respect the lifecycle.** Email reflects where a contact is now, not where they were at capture:
- An attendee never gets a cold "discover Convene X" invite again.
- A declined contact gets nothing for that event, only the next relevant table in their sector.
- A "Do not contact" or unsubscribed contact never enters any sequence, including imports.
- Delegates and sponsor contacts never share a sequence.

**Sequence design spec** (fill in for every sequence and file it with the draft copy):
```
## [Sequence name] - Design spec
Trigger: [added to invite list / registered / waitlisted / attended / no-show / date-based]
Segment: [e.g. Sector=Cyber AND Seniority in (C-level, VP-Director) AND Lifecycle=Invited]
Exclusions: [already registered / in another active sequence / sponsor contacts / suppressed]
Consent basis: [consent / legitimate interest (corporate address) / soft opt-in]

| # | Timing (from event date) | Subject A / B | Focus | CTA | Exit if |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | T-35 days | ... | Invitation + table question | Request your seat | Registers / declines |

Exit conditions: registers, declines, unsubscribes, hard bounce, complaint, out-of-office with leave dates past the event, duplicate.
Targets: CTR, reply rate, registrations (see metrics below).
```
No sequence runs indefinitely; every one has explicit exits.

**B2B event lifecycle map** (sequences Amara owns or templates; timing always works back from the event date):
1. **Invitation** (lifecycle = Invited): T-35 invite from the host/Morgan (personal-feel, plain text); T-28 follow-up with the table question and who the room is for (seniority and sector, never names); T-21 last personal nudge. Exit on register or decline. Personal outreach waves run alongside, per Leo's runbook.
2. **Confirmation** (transactional): immediate on registration. Existing Part 1 pre-event nurture then applies (T-14, T-2, day before).
3. **"Last seats"**: T-10 to T-7, only to Invited contacts who clicked but did not register. Honest scarcity only: never claim seats are limited unless they are (no fake urgency).
4. **Waitlist**: on joining, an immediate acknowledgement that sets expectations; release email the moment a seat frees up, with a clear 24-48 hour hold; a T-2 "we couldn't fit you this time, first call on the next [sector] table" email if no seat came up. Waitlisted contacts get priority in the next sector invite.
5. **No-show**: T+1, warm, no guilt: "Sorry we missed you. Here are the three themes the table raised." Mark as no-show; one more chance at the next invite, then deprioritise.
6. **Post-event** (Attended): existing 48-hour thank-you and community invite stand. Add a T+14 takeaways email (anonymised, Chatham House style) and a T+30 "next table in your sector" invite. No selling, ever, per Part 1.
7. **Member nurture** (Attended 1+ or Member): monthly, 200-300 words, the sector brief's top challenge, one Journal link, one upcoming table. Aim for 8-12 touches over 60-90 days between events, not weekly blasts.
8. **Reactivation** (no click/reply/registration in 180 days): 2-3 emails over 14-21 days. "Still the right person for [sector] tables?" with a one-click preference update. No response > suppress from marketing.

**Behavioural triggers** (only where the Convene X CRM tracking and consent allow it):
- Event page visited, no registration > 24h > a gentle personal note from the host (not an automated "we saw you").
- Registration form started, not finished > 4h > "finish your request" reminder.
- Clicked the invite, didn't register > 48h > the "last seats" or topic follow-up.
- Attended > 7 days > the feedback form (feeds Nadia's synthesis).

**Keep transactional and marketing separate.** Confirmations, joining instructions and calendar holds are transactional: no promotions, no next-event plugs inside them. Marketing content goes in marketing emails with an unsubscribe link.

**Clicks and replies over opens.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens, so opens are directional only. Judge sends on CTR, click-to-open, reply rate and registrations. For senior audiences, a plain-text reply ("Yes, count me in") is a conversion.

**Subject lines:** write 3 variants, test A/B on 10-20% of the segment where the list is large enough (roughly 400+ recipients), otherwise log the choice and compare across events. Specific beats clever: "Cyber leaders' dinner, 14 Oct: third-party risk" over "You're invited!".

**UK GDPR / PECR consent checklist** (run before every new list is mailed; Eleanor/Legal Compliance confirms anything unclear, check current ICO guidance as the rules change):
- [ ] **Corporate vs individual subscriber.** PECR lets us email employees at corporate addresses (limited companies, LLPs, public bodies) without prior consent, provided we identify ourselves and offer an easy opt-out. Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals and need consent or the soft opt-in.
- [ ] **UK GDPR still applies** to named people at corporate addresses. Record the lawful basis (usually legitimate interest, with a short legitimate interests assessment on file) and make the privacy notice available.
- [ ] **Soft opt-in** only for people whose details we got in the course of a sale or negotiation of our own similar service, who were given the chance to opt out at collection and in every message.
- [ ] **Bought or scraped lists:** don't mail without Legal's sign-off; never assume consent from an import.
- [ ] **Consent records:** date, method, source (form URL or event), wording shown, scope. Withdrawable in one click.
- [ ] **Every marketing email:** clear sender identity, working unsubscribe, a List-Unsubscribe header, and UK company details in the footer (registered name, number, registered office).
- [ ] **Tracking:** flag to Legal whether open/click tracking needs mentioning in the privacy notice or consent; the ICO treats tracking pixels as PECR storage/access technology.
- [ ] **Retention:** anonymise or delete contacts after 24 months with no engagement unless there's a documented reason to keep them.
- [ ] **Suppression list** honoured across every tool (the Convene X CRM, Zoho Mail manual sends, Andrew's outreach).

**Deliverability checklist** (the convenexevents sending domain; check once, then quarterly):
- [ ] SPF, DKIM and DMARC records in DNS and aligned with the From domain (DMARC starting at p=none with reporting, then tightening).
- [ ] Complaint rate under 0.10% (0.30% is the Gmail/Yahoo hard limit).
- [ ] Hard bounces removed within 24 hours; soft bounces suppressed after 3-5 failures.
- [ ] Role addresses (info@, admin@) suppressed from invites.
- [ ] Google Postmaster Tools set up once volume justifies it.
- [ ] Test every new template in Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, on mobile, and check the unsubscribe flow end to end.

**Launch a new sequence carefully.** Send to 10-20% of the segment first, watch bounces and complaints for 24 hours, then release the rest. After 7-14 days review results; after 30 days (or one full event cycle) review sequence-level registrations and adjust.

**Metrics** (working targets for a B2B invite list; benchmarks are context, not guarantees):
| Metric | Good | Alert |
|---|---|---|
| Invite CTR | > 5% | < 2% |
| Click-to-open | > 10% | < 5% |
| Reply rate (personal-feel invites) | > 5% | < 1% |
| Invite > registration | Track vs previous event | Falling two events running |
| Unsubscribe rate | < 0.3% | > 0.5% |
| Complaint rate | < 0.05% | > 0.10% |
| Hard bounce rate | < 0.5% | > 1% |
| Show rate (registered > attended) | Track; feeds the seats x 1.3 rule in the runbook | Below previous event |
System targets: every active contact sits in at least one lifecycle segment; every lifecycle stage above has a drafted sequence.

**Conflict to note:** the launch model in the email funnel addition suggests 2-3 emails a day in launch week. For senior delegates under PECR/UK GDPR and the "never feel chased" rule, keep delegate lists to at most one email per event wave; the heavy cadence, if used at all, is only for opted-in lead-magnet subscribers, with Freya's approval.

## Email funnel strategy — integrated from LinkedIn growth playbook

Reference: `00-overview/linkedin-growth-playbook.md`

### Core principle
70% of revenue comes from email across multiple proven LinkedIn creators. LinkedIn builds the audience. Email converts them. The post is not where money gets made — the follow-up is.

### Lead Magnet Design
A lead magnet should be so good people would feel stupid saying no. It condenses all the micro-tips from content into one comprehensive resource.

For CX, lead magnets could be:
- "The Executive Dinner Playbook: How B2B Companies Are Replacing Conference Booths with Curated C-Suite Conversations"
- "7 Proven Formats for Reaching Senior Decision-Makers (Without Cold Outbound)"
- A case study compilation showing delegate quality and pipeline outcomes

### Welcome Sequence (3-5 emails, automated)

1. **Lead magnet delivery + thank you** — send the resource, set expectations for what's coming
2. **Origin story** — who the founder is (respecting anonymity rule — frame as "our team"), why CX exists, what problem sparked it. Build likability.
3. **Case study** — proof the solution works. Before/after and specific results from Convene X's own events only; no past-employer clients or results
4. **Educational email** — solve one specific problem. E.g. "Why your conference booth isn't reaching the people who actually sign the cheques"
5. **Soft CTA** — "For those interested in exploring whether a curated executive dinner could work for your pipeline, here's where to start." Link to Cal.com booking or enquiry form.

### Weekly Newsletter Strategy

- Follow the SMPV: every newsletter must connect to skill (executive events) + market (B2B marketing leaders) + problem (senior-buyer access)
- Use trending topics combined with CX's proven frameworks — don't just repeat LinkedIn posts
- Not every email sells — build goodwill between launches
- Check the vibes: what's the market feeling right now? Address current pain points.
- Long-form storytelling works better in email than on LinkedIn — more room for nuance, vulnerability, and depth

### Launch Model (for future offers — events, packages, partnerships)

**3-month cycle:** Build goodwill with free value for ~3 months, then run a 2-week launch.

**Week 1 — Objection handling (no direct selling yet):**
- Storytelling: why executive events changed the game for a specific company
- Case study: "How [company] generated [X] qualified meetings from a single dinner"
- Authority proof: press mentions, speaking engagements, delegate testimonials
- Light tease + waitlist: "Something new is coming. Join the waitlist here."

**Week 2 — Announce and sell hard:**
- First line of email announces the offer — don't bury it
- Daily case studies and testimonials
- Be visceral about what you're selling — don't shy away
- 2-3 emails per day during launch week (rotate: educational, story, case study)

### Email-specific rules for CX
- Proof claim framing rule applies in email too: "our team / across their careers"
- Founder anonymity rule applies — no "Nathan" in email copy
- All email sequences go through Felix brand review → Freya sign-off → Nathan approval before activation
- the Convene X CRM marketing email tools are the send mechanism
